# Objection Handling

*The objections hosts will raise, the ones Tesla's own executives will raise, and the answer to each. Answers are meant to be said out loud in one breath. Where the honest answer is "that is a real risk," it says so and gives the mitigation.*

---

## From hosts

**"We're running a hyperscaler process. Why take less than $1.8M a megawatt?"**
Because you are not giving up $1.8M. That rent pays for a $12M-a-megawatt shell and two years of construction risk. In our structure you fund $2M and rent starts in months. Run the yield on your capital under both and ours is higher. And nothing stops you signing the hyperscaler lease on the rest of the campus; ten-year terms and removable equipment are the point.

**"Tesla has never been a data center tenant. Why would you be a reliable one?"**
Tesla runs more than 225 MW of training compute at Giga Texas today and has said publicly it will reach 400 MW by year-end. The company has guided roughly $20B of 2026 capex at AI infrastructure, funded from operating cash flow. The lease would carry a Tesla, Inc. guarantee. Your lenders can underwrite that.

**"We don't want a tenant that competes with us for Megapacks."**
The opposite. Tesla controls the allocation, and a Megapod site is the one place Tesla has a reason to put the host at the front of the line. The term sheet will include a priority allocation for your firming needs. No other tenant can offer that.

**"Why a self-contained module? Our tenants want Tier III halls."**
Training compute does not need Tier III. It needs power, cooling, fiber, and ride-through, and the battery supplies the ride-through. The module is why your capital is $2M instead of $12M and why we can be running in months. If you want to build and lease a hall, we can price that, but then you are in a hyperscaler process on hyperscaler timelines.

**"Our interconnection is curtailable / Partial Capacity Load / subject to SB6."**
That is exactly where this fits. With 20 to 40 MWh per block we can honor a curtailment call without dropping the job, run as a controllable load, and smooth the training swings your utility is worried about. A battery-backed tenant protects your classification; a bare GPU hall threatens it.

**"Our lenders won't allow a new tenant without consent."**
We expect that. We will provide a direct agreement and non-disturbance language, and we will accept a security interest in the Tesla-funded site improvements. The River Bend and Beacon Point leases were financed with investment-grade project bonds; we will paper to that standard.

**"What happens to the Megapods if Tesla walks?"**
They are trade fixtures. You have a purchase option at depreciated book value, we have a removal obligation with a decommissioning bond, and the termination fee steps down from 24 to 6 months of fees after year five. You are better protected than under a hall lease where the tenant leaves you a bespoke building.

**"Why not just sell us Megapacks?"**
We will, and that conversation is separate. But a sale leaves your megawatts earning mining margin until a tenant arrives. The tenancy fills the megawatts now and brings the Megapacks with it.

**"We've heard Tesla moves slowly on commercial agreements."**
Term sheet in ten business days of an NDA and the block data. We will put that in writing.

**"What about Bitcoin's optics for Tesla?"**
Tesla holds Bitcoin on its balance sheet and has since 2021. The transaction is a lease of powered land and electrical capacity, not a mining venture. Optics are not a constraint for us.

## From Tesla's own leadership

**"Megapack is sold out for 2027. Why divert units to our own tenancy?"**
Because each 100 MW Megapod site pulls through 200 MWh of internal demand at cost and opens a sales channel into the host's remaining capacity, which is often several hundred more megawatts. The program needs a defined carve-out, roughly 2 GWh over two years, which is well under 2% of planned nameplate capacity once Megafactory Texas ramps.

**"Why not just build at Giga Texas?"**
We are. Cortex 1 and 2 are at 225 MW and heading to 400 MW. The next 400 MW needs a new switchyard and more ERCOT interconnection at a time when the large-load queue is 474 GW. Hosting adds capacity in 2027 instead of 2029, on other people's interconnections, in several ISOs, with no stranded shell if the chip roadmap moves the compute.

**"These counterparties are leveraged crypto miners."**
Some are. That is why the structure is a site fee on reserved MW with Tesla owning every asset from the switchgear in, step-in rights with the utility, and non-disturbance from the host's lenders. If a host fails, Tesla keeps the equipment and the power, and the site has a replacement landlord within weeks. The pattern is proven: Google, AMD, and Nvidia have all taken warrants and provided backstops to these same companies.

**"Isn't this what SpaceX and xAI do? Why would Tesla do it independently?"**
SpaceX sells compute to third parties from Memphis. Tesla needs compute for FSD, Optimus, and robotaxi. This program is Tesla-only, at arm's length, with no related-party element, which is what the board and shareholders asked for after the November 2025 vote.

**"Why not partner with a neocloud and rent GPUs?"**
GPU cloud costs run roughly $9–10M per MW-year all-in (IREN–Microsoft). Hosting our own compute at $1M per MW-year in site fees plus owned hardware is cheaper over any horizon longer than two years, and it keeps the hardware roadmap in our hands.

**"The Submer 'MegaPod' trademark is a problem."**
Possibly, for the marketing name. It is not a problem for a site agreement, which can describe the equipment generically. Legal should clear the naming before any public announcement.

**"What if ERCOT or Texas changes the rules again?"**
That is a real risk, which is why the target list spans ERCOT, SPP, MISO, PJM, and NYISO, and why the battery is in every block. Texas SB6 already gives ERCOT curtailment authority over large loads; a battery-backed, controllable load is the design that survives whatever comes next.
