# Walmart US distribution centers ranked by likely substation capacity (10–20 MW Megapod screen)

Generated by `scripts/build_ranking.py` from `data/dc_roster.csv` (search-cited roster), the HIFLD Electric Substations layer (public-domain DHS/ORNL vintage mirrored on GitHub; taps, risers and dead-ends removed), the HIFLD retail service-territory layer (utility names), and `data/known_dedicated_subs.csv` (press-confirmed dedicated substations newer than the HIFLD vintage).

**Score** = voltage weight (345 kV+ = 5, 230 = 4, 115/138/161 = 3, 69 = 2, <69 = 1, not stated in HIFLD = 1) × proximity weight (<0.4 km = 3, <0.8 km = 2, <3 km = 1) + 1 if open year ≥ 2022. `NONE` means no HIFLD substation within 3 km. **Because every DC in this run is geocoded to a ZIP or city centroid, the <0.4 km and <0.8 km tiers are not applied: the proximity weight is capped at 1 and the voltage weight is taken from the strongest substation within 3 km of the centroid.** The script applies the full tiers automatically once a row has address-level coordinates (`geocode_precision = address`). The only row scored on the full tiers is Belvidere, IL, where a cited ComEd release confirms a dedicated 138 kV substation on the adjoining parcel.

**Read the geocode precision column before trusting any distance.** The environment could not reach Nominatim or the Census geocoder, so every DC is placed at its ZIP-code centroid (or city centroid when no ZIP was found). That is typically 1–5 km from the actual parcel, so distances are a neighbourhood screen, not a parcel measurement, and `on_parcel_likely` is only TRUE where a cited utility release confirms a dedicated substation.

Roster size: 160 facilities. 139 have a HIFLD substation within 3 km of the geocoded point.

## What HIFLD can't tell us

- **Spare capacity.** HIFLD records location and voltage class only. It has no transformer MVA ratings, no loading, and no hosting-capacity figure. A 138 kV substation can be fully subscribed; a 69 kV one can have 20 MVA to spare. Only the utility's interconnection or load study answers this.
- **Customer-owned vs utility-owned.** The HIFLD substations layer has no owner field. The `sub_owner` column is the retail service territory the substation sits in, i.e. the utility (or utilities) that would serve a new load there, not necessarily the owner of the substation. Some substations near DCs are customer-owned (industrial park, rail, or the DC itself), which changes who you negotiate with and whether the capacity is contractually committed.
- **Feeder and transformer loading.** Whether the distribution feeders from that substation to the DC parcel are loaded at 40% or 95% is invisible here, as is whether the DC takes service at distribution or transmission voltage.
- **Vintage.** The public HIFLD layer stopped updating around 2022 and the open portal closed in 2025. New substations built for 2023+ DCs (Belvidere IL, Lancaster TX, Wellford SC, Stockton CA, Pilesgrove NJ, Carnesville GA) are not in it; only Belvidere has a cited utility release, so the others may be under-ranked.
- **Geocode precision.** See above. A DC that geocodes to a ZIP centroid 3 km from its parcel can show NONE while a substation sits across the road from the actual site, and vice versa.
- **Walmart's own on-site electrical.** Many large DCs have a customer-owned step-down yard or a utility 'dedicated' sub inside the fence that HIFLD names generically (UNKNOWN*) or omits (<69 kV).

## Questions to ask Walmart facilities / energy procurement for each top site

Generic to every site:

1. What is the service voltage and contracted demand (kW) at this DC today, and who is the serving utility and rate schedule?
2. Is the substation feeding the DC utility-owned or Walmart-owned? If Walmart-owned, what is the transformer nameplate (MVA) and the current peak load?
3. Has the utility stated available capacity at the serving substation or feeder (MW) for additional load, and is there a pending interconnection or load-addition study?
4. How much developable land is inside the fence or on the adjoining parcel (acres), and is any of it already reserved for solar, EV charging, or expansion?
5. Does the DC's existing onsite generation (solar, battery, backup gensets) or Walmart's PPA portfolio constrain adding a 10–20 MW behind-the-meter or co-located load?
6. Who at Walmart Realty / Energy signs on a ground lease or easement for a co-located battery + compute installation, and what is the approval path?
